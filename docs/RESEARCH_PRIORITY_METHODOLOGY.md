# Research Priority Methodology

The Research Priority Score allocates research effort. It does not measure institutional performance, policy quality or financial strength.

Each institution receives four integer scores from 1 to 5:

1. Strategic relevance
2. Comparability potential
3. Official-source availability
4. Value of closing the research gap

The total ranges from 4 to 20. Active records are high at 16–20, medium at 11–15 and low at 4–10. A record may be marked `deferred` when critical official sources cannot currently be accessed; ASKRINDO is the current example. Deferral preserves its research value while preventing unsupported data collection.

Field research is sequenced separately: legal, oversight, funding, delivery, target and risk-sharing fields are Tier A; publications, governance and network fields are Tier B; geographic scope is Tier C. Scores and rationales are reviewed and date-stamped in `src/data/research-priority.ts`.
